# Writing — clear and accountable

- Lead with what changed and why it matters. Use plain language, precise names, and the detail the reader needs to act.
- Comments explain intent, constraints, and non-obvious behavior. Document public contracts; do not narrate obvious code or repeat rules in every file.
- Errors explain what failed and what the user can do next. Define specialist terms when the audience needs them.
- PRs describe the final problem, behavior, validation, and material risk. Reports distinguish measured results from estimates and open questions.
- Attribute audit findings and work artifacts to the actual contributor/session when known; do not invent model names, versions, review verdicts, or collective approval.
